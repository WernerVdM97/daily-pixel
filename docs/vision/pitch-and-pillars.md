# The Warden's Oak

> *A year is a long time to carry an ember. But it's longer still to carry one alone.*

---

You are a group of travelers who have never met. You live ordinary lives in ordinary villages — until the smoke rises from Stonebridge, a star falls in the east, and every well in the kingdom runs dry on the same morning. Something is wrong. Something is waking.

Each day, the world advances. You get two rolls. You travel, rest, scout, fight, talk, flee. Some days you forget — and your character sits by the fire at the Warden's Oak, waiting. Some weeks you disappear entirely, and the world moves on without you, cruelly, indifferently.

After a few weeks, paths cross. A fellowship forms — not because destiny demands it, but because you all saw the same smoke, heard the same rumour, and a warden who never speaks finally looked east.

Several months later, whatever has been waking will arrive. Whether the fellowship is still standing — that depends on dice rolls each day (extra on weekends)

This is a game about memory. About the spaces between. About a tree at a crossroads and the names carved into its bark, some so old the bark has swallowed them halfway.

## Thematic DNA

| Tag             | Weight       | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **mud**         | foundational | The original multi-user dungeon. A shared, persistent text world explored through typed commands, where players coexist in rooms drawn in words and the world keeps turning whether you're logged in or not. The Warden's Oak is a MUD distilled to one ritual a day.                                 |
| **frieren**     | anchor       | Time is the central tension. Bonds form and fray across real calendar months. The yearly reset gives everything weight — knowing it ends makes the daily ritual matter.                                                                                                                              |
| **pixelart**    | visual     | The game is rendered in ASCII art. Low-res deliberate, not low-effort. Every scene, character, and item is text you can scroll on a phone screen.                                                                                                                                                    |
| **dnd**         | mechanical | Two daily rolls. Rest and recovery. Party composition matters. The dice are visible and the stakes are real.                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **lotr**        | structural | Fellowship of many. Splitting paths. A greater unknown threat that looms without being fought directly. The yearly convergence.                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **castlevania** | tonal      | Gothic fantasy. The threat should feel like Dracula's castle on the horizon — distant, impossible, slowly approaching. Pixel monsters.                                                                                                                                                               |
| **animé**       | emotional  | Friendship-as-mechanic. Dramatic narrative framing. Characters have feelings and the game names them. "Lina seems distant today."                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **maplestory**  | nostalgic  | The social grind as ritual. A central hub where players idle. Rare drops as bragging rights. The game *feels like* Henesys at 2am in 2006.                                                                                                                                                           |
| **fable**       | simulation | NPCs live daily lives independent of players. They work, earn, spend, grieve, celebrate. The world breathes — shops open and close, rumors spread, fortunes rise and fall. A blacksmith who had a good week might commission a new sign. A farmer whose crop failed might drink alone at the tavern. |
