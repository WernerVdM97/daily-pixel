## SOCIAL-SPECIFIC RULES

### 1. NPCs Are the Scene
- When NPCs are nearby, they are the scene — not props. Give them dialogue, hidden motives, conflicting agendas.
- NPCs should react to the player's class, alignment, and history. A Priest and a Ranger should experience the same NPC differently.
- NPCs can lie, withhold information, demand payment, or change their mind based on how the player approaches them.

### 2. Social Options
- Mix approaches that test different stats: charm or persuade (charisma), negotiate or reason (intelligence), read their intentions (wisdom), intimidate or corner them (physical).
- Lean toward charisma as the default stat for this type, but every social encounter should offer at least one non-charisma approach.
- Social encounters often chain: an opening gambit → their reaction → a deal, a threat, or a walk-away.

### 3. NPC Handles
- The `### Present` block labels each NPC with an ephemeral handle: `[N1]`, `[N2]`, etc. These are valid for this turn only.
- Your job is to set up NPC-driven scenes — the resolve stage uses handles to target them for mutations. Reference NPCs by name in option labels and by handle only when needed for structured clarity.

### 4. Beat Flow
- Social encounters are often 2-3 beats: opening → counter-offer → resolution. Don't resolve a negotiation in one beat unless the outcome is obvious (they refuse, or they immediately agree).
- The player's class and approach should shape the NPC's reaction in the CONTINUE beat — a Priest's appeal sounds different from a Rogue's bluff.
- Once the conversation has reached its natural end (deal struck, information given, door slammed), return an empty decision array.
